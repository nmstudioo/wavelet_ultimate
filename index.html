Part 01 · Foundations

Tuning Philosophy

Wavelet's controls are correction tools first, coloration tools second. Every recommendation on this page — and every preset in it — follows the same order of operations:

  1. 1

    Identify what the transducer objectively cannot do, or does poorly

    This is a physical fact — measurable, testable with the processing turned off — not a preference.

  2. 2

    Correct only for that. Nothing more

    Adding gain or effects beyond what's needed to fix a known weakness increases clipping risk and makes future problems harder to diagnose.

  3. 3

    Add taste only as a separate, clearly-labeled layer, after the corrective baseline is settled

    This guide's presets stop at the corrective baseline; personal coloration is left to you, deliberately.

Working rule — one tool owns one correction

Never let AutoEQ, a manual Graphic EQ, and a Bass Tuner all fight over the same frequency region — each layer compounds gain and makes it impossible to tell which one caused a problem when something sounds off. This is exactly what was happening in the original KZ EDX Pro setup, where a manual EQ was re-boosting the same regions AutoEQ was already correcting.

Working rule — a "final" preset is a validated hypothesis, not a ruling

It's the best current answer given the evidence gathered so far — driver behavior, genre testing, platform quirks. New evidence should update it. Nothing in this guide is meant to be defended past the point where a listening test contradicts it.

Part 02 · Foundations

Methodology

How the conclusions in this guide were actually reached — so you can repeat the process yourself when something new comes up.

  1. 1

    Isolate the variable

    When something sounds wrong, test with Wavelet fully OFF before touching any single setting. This separates DSP-caused issues from hardware or source-material issues.

    How the Redmi 12's high-volume buzzing was confirmed as a hardware ceiling, not an EQ problem — it persisted with Wavelet completely disabled

  2. 2

    Attribute cause correctly before prescribing a fix

    A symptom like "songs sound slow" can come from limiter pumping, a CPU buffer underrun, or OS background throttling — three unrelated causes with three unrelated fixes. Reason through each rather than assuming the most recently changed setting is the culprit.

  3. 3

    Test against representative material, not one track

    A limiter setting validated only on pop/vocal music won't necessarily hold on a differently-mastered genre. Both need testing before a preset is called "done."

    Exactly how the Redmi 12's Limiter was found to work fine on standard tracks but pump on hard rock/metal

  4. 4

    Prefer subtraction over addition

    Removing a driver's known weak points is lower-risk than boosting to compensate for them — boosting adds gain, headroom pressure, and clipping risk for a result the driver often can't cleanly reproduce anyway.

  5. 5

    Treat platform behavior as a variable, not a constant

    Android's audio effect implementation differs by OEM and OS version. A slider's real adjustable range, or whether an effect is even available, can differ from device to device and can change after an update. Verify live rather than trusting a remembered value.

Part 03 · Objective

Hardware Limitations

Every transducer has a frequency range and an excursion budget it can reproduce cleanly. Pushing it further with EQ doesn't recover missing sound — it adds distortion. This section is about physical fact, not taste.

Redmi 12 (built-in speaker)
  • A single, mono, bottom-firing driver — there is no stereo image to preserve or widen, by construction.
  • Its diaphragm excursion budget is small. Deep bass content pushes it past clean linear movement, producing audible buzzing/rattling at high volume. This is a mechanical ceiling — confirmed reproducible even with Wavelet fully disabled — not something any EQ or limiter setting removes.
  • Because of that ceiling, the correction philosophy here is subtractive: remove the frequencies the driver can't move cleanly, rather than boosting to compensate for their absence (which would just turn into distortion — "fake bass").
KZ EDX Pro (dynamic-driver IEM)
  • Seated directly in the ear canal — high sensitivity, and no meaningful physical excursion limit at normal phone output levels. Clipping/distortion risk from the hardware itself is effectively negligible in this use case.
  • Its deviations from a neutral response are tuning choices baked into the driver and shell design — measured and catalogued in AutoEQ's database — not excursion failures.
  • The correction tool that fits this is a measured EQ curve (AutoEQ), not a protective one (Limiter).

Part 04 · Objective fact, subjective consequence

Genre Dependence

Mastering loudness and dynamic range vary systematically by genre. Heavily produced, high-average-loudness genres (metal, hard rock, EDM) leave little headroom between average and peak level. Quieter, more dynamic genres (acoustic, jazz, classical, much vocal-forward pop) leave considerably more.

Acoustic / Jazz / Classical Wide headroom
Vocal-forward Pop Moderate headroom
Hard Rock / Metal / EDM Little headroom

↑ Illustrative comparison of typical headroom between average and peak loudness by genre — not measured dB values.

A limiter's threshold is a fixed dB value. Against a genre that already sits close to that value most of the time, the limiter engages almost continuously instead of only on rare peaks — producing audible pumping that the same setting doesn't cause on more dynamic material. This was diagnosed directly on the Redmi 12: identical Limiter values sounded clean on standard tracks and chaotic on hard rock/metal.

Consequence

There is no single limiter configuration that is simultaneously correct for a heavily compressed genre and a dynamic one. That's not a flaw in any one setting — it's an inherent property of applying one fixed threshold/ratio/attack/release combination to material with very different loudness profiles. Practically, this means the Limiter is best treated as a context-dependent tool with more than one validated configuration, not one universal number. See Limiter Best Practices.

Instead of guessing an EQ curve, Wavelet applies a correction curve someone already measured for your exact model, targeting a neutral, "Harman-style" reference sound. Headphones/earbuds only — there's no database entry for phone speakers.

Feature 03

Equal Loudness

Implements the ISO 226 standard — ears hear bass/treble as weaker at low volume than high volume. This auto-compensates so tonal balance feels consistent at any volume.

Parameters
Volume threshold
The level above which compensation stops changing; set it near your normal listening volume.

Generic example

Threshold at 50% device volume — below it, Wavelet gently lifts bass/treble; above it, EQ stays as configured.

KZ EDX Pro OFF

Left off deliberately — the current AutoEQ-only baseline doesn't use it. It's a legitimate optional addition if your typical listening volume varies a lot day to day, but it would shift the tonal balance underneath the already-precise AutoEQ curve.

Redmi 12 Speaker OFF

Not part of that baseline either. You already hand-tuned the curve for one listening level, so turning this on would start shifting bass/treble again as volume changes — worth testing carefully rather than enabling by default.

Feature 08

Attenuator

The first stage of processing — a pre-gain trim that creates headroom before anything else happens, so later boosts don't clip.

Parameters
Automatic attenuation
Wavelet calculates combined peak gain and auto-sets the trim.
Attenuation
Manual dB value you set yourself.
Redmi 12 Speaker OFF

Your final Graphic EQ curve is net-negative (far more cuts than boosts), so there's no extra gain that needs compensating — turning it on would just lower volume for no benefit. Revisit only if a future EQ addition adds positive gain somewhere.

KZ EDX Pro OFF

Feature 09

Limiter

Catches transient peaks that slip past the Attenuator's average headroom. Whether to use it at all is now treated as a risk question, not a default — full mechanics and both Redmi 12 tiers are broken out separately in Limiter Best Practices (Part 6).

Parameters
Attack time
Reaction speed. Fast (1–2ms) = effective but can be audible. Slow (10ms+) = natural, but front edge of peaks may sneak through.
Release time
How long reduction holds. Fast = audible "pumping." Slow = smoother, can smear into the next transient.
Ratio
Reduction strength above threshold (4:1 = a 4dB-over peak comes out 1dB over).
Threshold
Level where limiting starts — also determines how often the limiter engages against a given genre's typical loudness.
Post-gain
Makeup gain added back after limiting.
Redmi 12 Speaker OPTIONAL

OFF by default. Not a mandatory always-on effect — a deliberate choice for high-volume listening or hot-mastered genres, with two candidate configurations depending on material (see Genre Dependence).

Attack 4ms Release 100ms* Ratio 5:1 Threshold −2dB Post-gain 0dB

KZ EDX Pro OFF

Part 5, cont'd · System

General / System Settings

Not part of the effects chain — these control how Wavelet attaches to your audio.

Buffer Size

Low = less precise, lower latency (good for video). High = more precise, higher latency (better for music). Setting it beyond what the processor can compute in real time produces audible clicking, cracking, or stutter — which can be mistaken for "slow" playback.

Both setups: Since you're listening to music only (not video) on both the Redmi 12 speaker and through the KZ EDX Pro, a higher buffer (4096 samples) is the right default. Drop it only if you hear crackling.

Legacy Mode

Off by default — a targeted compatibility fix for apps Wavelet can't auto-detect, not a default-on setting. Turning it on disables Reverberation and, on some devices, other effects.

Both setups: Leave OFF unless Wavelet's notification shows it isn't attached to your music app during playback. Neither setup showed a compatibility issue requiring it.

Enhanced Session Detection

Experimental, Android 10+. Actively watches for any audio session instead of waiting for a broadcast. Requires Developer Options + USB debugging + a one-time DUMP permission grant via ADB.

Both setups: Not needed for either — this is a last-resort fix, only worth touching if Legacy Mode alone fails to get a specific app working.

Background & Battery Permission

Aggressive OEM battery managers — particularly MIUI/HyperOS on Xiaomi devices like the Redmi 12 — can intermittently suspend Wavelet's background processing, producing symptoms indistinguishable from a bad DSP setting.

Redmi 12 specifically: grant Wavelet unrestricted background/battery permission, and rule this out before attributing a playback problem to an EQ or Limiter value (see Android Quirks).

Input Gain

A newer addition in recent Wavelet versions, split out as its own manual pre-gain stage alongside the Attenuator.

Both setups: Not part of either preset — both setups achieved correct headroom through the Attenuator alone, so there was no need to reach for this.

Limiter Best Practices

Mechanical recap (objective)
Attack
Reaction speed. Fast (1–2ms) = effective but audible; slow (10ms+) = natural, but the front edge of a peak may slip through.
Release
How long gain reduction holds. Fast = audible "pumping"; slow = smoother but can smear into the next transient.
Ratio
Reduction strength above threshold (a 4:1 ratio turns a 4dB-over peak into 1dB-over).
Threshold
The level where limiting starts; also determines how often the limiter engages against a given genre's typical loudness (see Genre Dependence).
Post-gain
Makeup gain added back after limiting.

Whether to use it at all is a risk question, not a default

A limiter is protection proportional to actual physical risk. Apply it where a real excursion/clipping risk exists — a small speaker driven at high volume; skip it where that risk is negligible — an efficient IEM at phone output levels. Running one anyway adds DSP load and complexity for no corrective benefit, which is exactly what happened when it was removed from the KZ EDX Pro setup.

Redmi 12 — two validated tiers, not one universal setting

Part 4 (Genre Dependence) explains why one setting can't serve both cases.

Redmi 12 limiter tiers: parameters and when to use each tier.
Tier Attack Release Ratio Threshold Post-gain Use when
Standard 1ms 100ms 10:1 −2dB 0dB Typical, dynamically-mastered tracks — validated clean on vocal-forward/standard material
Dense / Hot-Master 4ms 100ms* 5:1 −2dB 0dB Hard rock, metal, EDM, or other heavily compressed genres — validated to remove the pumping/chaos the Standard tier caused on this material

*Release is shown at the device's currently observed ceiling — verify your own live-adjustable maximum rather than assuming this figure (see Android Quirks).

If pumping persists on the Dense tier

The next lever is Threshold, not Ratio or Attack — moving it to −3dB reduces how often the limiter engages in the first place, independent of how hard it squeezes when it does.

Part 7

Android / Wavelet Implementation Quirks

Some symptoms that look like bad tuning are actually platform behavior. Ruling these out is part of the Methodology.

Parameter quantization

Android's DynamicsProcessing API exposes fixed steps, not free continuous values. Dialing in "8.0:1" and observing it settle at "7.2:1" is the platform snapping to its nearest supported step — not a bug, and not a meaningful change in behavior (sub-1.0 ratio differences are inaudible).

Parameter range differences

The maximum adjustable Release time isn't guaranteed identical across devices, or even across time on the same device — an inconsistency was observed on the Redmi 12 between an originally-reported 120ms value and a later 100ms ceiling. Always verify the live-adjustable range on your actual device rather than trusting a remembered or documented figure.

Buffer size vs. real-time load

Setting Buffer Size beyond what the device's processor can compute in real time produces audible clicking, cracking, or stutter — which can be mistaken for "slow" playback. The fix is empirical: start high for better precision on music, step down only if artifacts appear.

OEM background process management

Aggressive battery managers — particularly MIUI/HyperOS on Xiaomi devices like the Redmi 12 — can intermittently suspend Wavelet's background processing, producing symptoms indistinguishable from a bad DSP setting. Rule this out (unrestricted background/battery permission) before attributing a playback problem to an EQ or Limiter value.

Legacy Mode trade-off

Enabling it for compatibility with a stubborn app disables Reverberation, and can disable other effects depending on device — a targeted compatibility fix, not a default-on setting.
